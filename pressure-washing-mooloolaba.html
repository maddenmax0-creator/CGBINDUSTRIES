Local Knowledge

Salt, Sun and Slippery Pavers

Mooloolaba is a beachside suburb, and a lot of outdoor living here happens on hard surfaces: pool surrounds, paved alfresco areas, entries and driveways, often within sight of the water. That is exactly where salt air, strong sun and humidity do the most damage to paving and unsealed concrete.

Pool surrounds and pavers go first. Algae and mould settle into the joints and the surface of the paving, turning it dark and slippery underfoot, and a wet pool surround is the last place you want a slip. Cleaning lifts the growth and the grime and brings the colour back. It is as much a safety job as a cosmetic one.

Housing here is a mix of older low-set homes, townhouses and apartment buildings, many with shared pool areas and common paths. That makes the first questions practical ones: who owns the surface, when can it be closed off, and where does the water run. For strata and body corporate areas we agree scope and access in advance.

Sealing is the follow-up that stops salt-laden air and algae taking hold so quickly again. Sealers are UV and salt-rated, and anti-slip options are available for wet areas.

Questions

Mooloolaba FAQs

—
Why do pavers go slippery so quickly near the beach?

Humidity and salt-laden air keep paving damp and give algae and mould a surface to grow on. Once the growth builds up, wet paving turns slippery, which is why pool surrounds and paths are worth cleaning before they get to that point.

—
Can you clean shared pool areas at a Mooloolaba unit complex?

Yes. Body corporate and strata areas are quoted per site, with access, timing and water supply agreed in advance so the work fits around residents.

—
Does sealing make pool surround paving less slippery?

Sealing protects the surface and makes it easier to keep clean. For wet areas where grip is the concern, we can quote an anti-slip coating as well. We talk through the options when we look at the surface.
