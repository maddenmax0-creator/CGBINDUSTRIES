Why It Matters

Why Stairs Go Slippery First

Stairs and landings sit in the places water lingers: under eaves, beside garden beds and in the shade of trees. Damp concrete grows moss and algae, and on a step that growth is not only unsightly. It is a genuine slip risk, especially after rain.

Retaining walls and fencing collect the same growth, plus soil and leaf litter washed down from above. Left alone, the staining sets in and gets harder to lift.

Stairs also need more care than a flat driveway. Gradient, handrails, garden beds next to the surface and where the runoff goes all affect how the job is done, which is why we look at the site, or photos of it, before we quote.

Once the surface is clean, sealing slows the regrowth because it sheds water instead of holding it, and an anti-slip coating is an option where grip is the concern.

Questions

Stair and Retaining Wall FAQs

—
Is moss on steps really a safety issue?

Yes. Moss and algae on wet steps are slippery, and the risk is highest on shaded stairs that never fully dry. Cleaning removes the growth, and an anti-slip coating can add grip if you want it.

—
Can you work on steep or awkward stairs?

Usually. Gradient matters less than access, so we check where we can set up and how far the hose has to run when we quote.

—
How do I stop the moss coming back?

You cannot stop it entirely on shaded concrete, but sealing after a clean slows the regrowth noticeably because the surface sheds water rather than holding it.
